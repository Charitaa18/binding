Binding

Design in general education

1.Designerly ways of knowing

Nigel Cross, 1982

Cross says design is its own way of knowing, with its own language. Science has numbers, the humanities have words, and design has modelling. So I tested that on his own text (which was fascinating but really long and hard to read at times). His writing stays exactly as he wrote it, but after every section I break it down into key arguments, quick summaries and diagrams, the way I take notes myself.

2.Graphic Design Education as a Liberal Art

Gunnar Swanson, 1994

Swanson argues that most design education isn’t really education. It’s job training. He says design should be taught as a liberal art, part of a general education, because it’s all about how information gets shared with people. If Cross says design is its own way of knowing, Swanson says that’s exactly why it belongs in every classroom.

3.A New Program for Graphic Design

David Reinfurt, 2019

Reinfurt actually did it. He taught design at Princeton, a university rather than an art school, to students from all kinds of majors. He says most of them won’t become designers, but they take what they learn into whatever else they study. He even says the book’s “form is also its argument,” and that’s exactly what I’m trying to do here.

Bringing them together

Cross gives me the theory, Swanson the argument, and Reinfurt the proof that it works. A notebook felt like the obvious place to bring all three together, because every student already has one. I just redesigned it so the information actually sticks instead of reading like endless words strapped together. This isn’t about making a reading look more aesthetic. Design is a way of thinking, and I think it can help you learn too!
